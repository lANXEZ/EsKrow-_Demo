What the buyer's app checks

  1. QR format and CRC. The CRC only catches damaged codes. Anyone can recompute it, so it proves nothing about who made the QR.
  2. EsKrow+ tag present. An ordinary PromptPay QR has no EsKrow+ data. The app warns that the payment is not protected.
  3. Signature from a pinned bank key. The app ships with the bank's public key. A changed amount or payee, or a QR the seller signed with their own key, fails here.
  4. Payee is the bank's escrow account.
  5. Not expired, and issued for this buyer. This stops a seller reusing an old, genuine QR from another deal.
  6. Deal still open. An online check with the bank, so a paid QR can't be paid twice.

What changes in production

  • The private key lives in the bank's HSM and signs on the server. The demo uses WebCrypto in this page with a non-extractable key.
  • Public keys are built into the app, with a key ID (kid) so keys can be rotated without breaking old app versions.
  • The QR stays EMVCo / Thai QR compatible. EsKrow+ data uses the unreserved template tags 80–82, so other bank apps can still read the payee, while the bank app can also verify the signature.
  • The deal ID is also the bill-payment reference, so the bank can match any incoming transfer to its deal.
  • All IDs, accounts and biller numbers here are fake and won't resolve in a real banking app.
